Tier-2 cities in India are urban centres ranked below the largest metropolitan economies in population, infrastructure and consumption. There is no single official list, but the term generally covers cities with populations between roughly half a million and four million, such as Indore, Coimbatore, Lucknow, Bhubaneswar, Kochi and Nagpur. Tier-1 usually refers to the eight to ten largest metros, including Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune, Kolkata and Ahmedabad.These cities have grown quickly as retail, education and healthcare spending rises and as manufacturing decentralises from the metros. Household income data shows a consuming class expanding faster in tier-2 centres than in saturated metro markets, which has drawn retailers, quick-service restaurants and online platforms to expand there. Land and wage costs remain lower than in the metros, though logistics networks are thinner and reliable commercial real estate can be scarce.For online grocery and e-commerce companies, a tier-2 entry decision depends on several measurable conditions: population density in target neighbourhoods, smartphone and digital payment penetration, average household income, the size of existing modern retail, and the cost of building delivery infrastructure from zero. Studies of past expansions note that companies entering too many cities at once have struggled, because each city needs local supply relationships, warehouse space and a minimum order volume before its operating costs can be covered. A phased entry, starting with the densest and most digitally active centres, is the pattern most commonly recommended in retail expansion literature.
