A dark store is a small warehouse that serves online orders only and accepts no walk-in customers. The format is used by grocery delivery platforms, pharmacies and prepared-meal services that need to pick and pack orders quickly within a city.The layout of a dark store is designed around picking speed rather than browsing. Racks are arranged by demand frequency, with the fastest-moving items placed near packing counters. Typical floor space ranges from two thousand to five thousand square feet, small enough to fit inside a residential or commercial block, which reduces the distance a delivery rider must travel. A single store generally stocks one to five thousand items, chosen from the platform's central catalogue based on local sales data.Labour inside a dark store is divided between pickers, who collect items against a digital order list, and packers, who assemble and seal the final bag. Some operators use mobile applications that sequence picking along an optimised walking route to reduce a picker's travel inside the store.The main cost lines are rent, labour and utilities, followed by inventory holding. Because the store is located in a dense area rather than an industrial zone, rent per square foot can be several times that of a suburban warehouse. Operators therefore measure a store's success by daily order count relative to these fixed costs, and a location that fails to reach sufficient volume is usually relocated or closed.
