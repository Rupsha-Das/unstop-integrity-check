Cold chain logistics refers to the transport and storage of perishable goods at controlled temperatures from the point of production to the point of sale. The chain covers refrigerated warehouses, temperature-controlled vehicles, insulated packaging and monitoring systems that record temperature throughout the journey.Fresh produce, dairy, frozen food, medicines and vaccines all require cold chains, though at different temperature ranges. Chilled goods are typically kept between two and eight degrees Celsius, while frozen goods remain below minus eighteen degrees. Each break in the chain, however brief, can shorten shelf life or make a product unsafe, so continuous monitoring is treated as a core requirement rather than an optional feature.In developing markets, cold chain infrastructure is uneven. Studies note that a large share of produce is lost between farm and retail because of missing refrigeration at collection points and long exposure to ambient temperatures during transport. Building capacity requires investment in pre-cooling facilities close to farms, reliable electricity at storage sites and a fleet of refrigerated vehicles for the final delivery stage.For online grocery retailers, the cold chain is often the largest capital cost after vehicles. A dark store handling fresh produce needs chillers, freezers and backup power, and every delivery bag may need insulation and gel packs. These costs raise the break-even order volume of a delivery operation and are a key reason fresh categories are harder to run profitably than shelf-stable ones.
