Quick commerce is a retail model in which online orders of groceries and household goods are delivered to customers within roughly thirty minutes. The format originated in India in the early 2020s and spread rapidly in dense urban neighbourhoods where orders can be fulfilled from small local warehouses positioned inside residential areas.Operators run networks of these small warehouses, commonly called dark stores, each holding a curated stock of two to four thousand fast-moving items. Because a store serves only a one to three kilometre radius, delivery routes are short and predictable. Riders typically complete a delivery in ten to fifteen minutes, and most platforms promise a window of thirty minutes or less. Order values are usually modest, since customers buy a few items for immediate need rather than a full weekly shop.The economics of quick commerce are difficult because of these small baskets. Revenue per order must cover picking labour, rider payment, fuel, rent on high-traffic retail space and packaging, all against an average basket that is often well below one thousand rupees. Platforms therefore try to raise order frequency, advertise supplier placements and attach delivery fees. Studies of the sector note that a mature store reaches profitability only after daily order volume crosses several hundred, which depends on population density and repeat purchase behaviour rather than on marketing spend alone.
